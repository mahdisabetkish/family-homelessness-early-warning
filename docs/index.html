Where is family homelessness about to rise?

A prospective test of statistical and machine learning methods for identifying areas where families with dependent children are about to become homeless. Everything runs on England's published homelessness returns and deprivation indices, and is validated on a year the models never saw.

Where prediction actually earns its place

Two questions put to the same dataset. Sorting a column answers the first. It does not answer the second.

Task A · Level a lookup

Will this authority be in the worst national quintile next year?

Task B · Escalation the real one

Will family homelessness rise by 25% or more next year?

The obvious heuristic is worse than useless. Ranking authorities by last year's increase scores an AUC of 0.35 on the escalation task, significantly below chance, because rises tend to mean-revert. A service acting on "it went up last year" is sending help to the places least likely to need it next.

What the uncertainty column is for. It is the spread across 100 Monte-Carlo dropout passes of the neural network. A high score the model is confident about and a high score it is essentially guessing at are different situations, and they deserve different responses from a service. How every model was validated

The protocol

  • Fitted on outcome years up to 2023–24, scored once on 2024–25. Nothing is tuned on the test year.
  • Every threshold is fixed from the training years alone, so no information from the scored year reaches its own label.
  • ROC-AUC carries a 2,000-draw percentile bootstrap interval.
  • Reported alongside PR-AUC, hit rate in a list of 30, and Brier.

What is deliberately absent

  • No heavy hyperparameter search. Every degree of freedom spent against a held-out year of 262 authorities buys noise.
  • No resampling to fix class imbalance. It distorts calibration; the loss is reweighted instead.
  • No stacking or ensembling. The differences between these models sit inside their confidence intervals, so combining them would be fitting to noise.

Labels and the split

Features come from year t, labels from year t+1 for the same authority. Escalation has fewer usable rows than level, because a relative change is undefined where the current rate is zero.

Level prevalence drifts upward instead of sitting at 20%. That is intended. The cutoff is a quantile of the training years, not of the year being scored, so a genuinely worsening national picture shows up as rising prevalence rather than being normalised away.

Method

Data

  • MHCLG statutory homelessness (H-CLIC) detailed local authority tables, financial years 2018–19 to 2024–25.
  • English Indices of Deprivation 2019, local authority summaries and LSOA-level scores.
  • Measures are selected by matching published header text rather than column position, so a layout change between releases raises an error instead of quietly shifting a feature. 251 of the 252 measure-years resolve. The single gap is real, because Section 21 notices were not collected in 2018–19.

Validation

  • Strictly temporal. Models are fitted on outcome years up to 2023–24 and scored once on 2024–25.
  • Every threshold, including the definition of the "worst quintile" label, is fixed from the training years alone, so no information from the scored year reaches its own label.
  • All ranking metrics carry 95% bootstrap intervals. With around 270 authorities and a small positive class, differences of a few points sit inside sampling noise.
  • Probabilities are isotonically calibrated. Whoever acts on a triage score will read it as a probability, so it had better be one.

Boundary and coverage reconciliation

Local authorities move. There were 326 districts in 2018–19 and 296 in 2024–25, while the deprivation indices are published on 2019 boundaries. Rather than dropping the affected years, the pipeline reconciles them.

  • Seven successor authorities (Buckinghamshire, the two Northamptonshires, the two Cumbrias, North Yorkshire and Somerset) have deprivation scores rebuilt by population-weighting their predecessors' LSOAs.
  • Fourteen districts abolished in April 2019 inherit their successor's score.
  • Every authority-year in the panel then carries a deprivation score, and the reconciliation is printed by the pipeline rather than hidden.

Data quality

Non-reporting is treated as a result in its own right. A silent zero and a genuine success look identical once they reach a summary table.

What this is, and what it is not

Limits

  • The published statistics are aggregate, so this predicts areas rather than households. It is a test of method, not a finding about any individual family.
  • Deprivation is fixed at 2019 while outcomes run to 2025.
  • Non-reporting is almost certainly not random.
  • Effect sizes are modest and the intervals are wide. An honest 2× lift is worth more than a polished 0.9.
  • Non-reporting has not been modelled.

What changes with household-level data

  • Monthly rather than annual, and at household level, so the horizon shortens from a year to a few weeks. That is the range in which prevention actually works.
  • Rent account trajectories, repairs, anti-social behaviour, contact history and tenancy events, all of which these returns record only after the fact.
  • Free-text case notes, where transformer-based feature extraction genuinely earns its place.
  • Children's outcomes, such as school moves and attendance.

How the code is built

Two layers

  • Twelve library modules hold the logic: parsing, measure selection, boundary reconciliation, feature construction, the models and their evaluation.
  • Nine stage scripts run them in order. Each reads one artefact, calls into the library, and writes the next.
  • Paths, financial years, the split and both thresholds are defined once, so the modelling stage and the dataset card cannot disagree about what the test year is.

What that is for

  • Seventy-eight tests, weighted towards the label construction and the metrics, because that is where a bug changes a published number without changing anything visible.
  • The model configurations on this page are read off the fitted pipelines, so a hyperparameter cannot change without the description changing with it.
  • A research project that leaves behind something nobody else can run has not left much.

Reproducing this

Nine numbered stages, run by a single command. Every figure and every number on this page is written by the pipeline rather than typed in.
