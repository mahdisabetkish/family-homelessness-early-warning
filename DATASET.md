## Labels and the split

Features come from year *t*, labels from year *t+1* for the same authority.

| Feature year | Outcome year | Split | Rows | Level positives | Escalation rows | Escalation positives |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2018-19 | 2019-20 | unused (no lag available) | 294 | 53 (18%) | 289 | 90 (31%) |
| 2019-20 | 2020-21 | train | 283 | 34 (12%) | 275 | 25 (9%) |
| 2020-21 | 2021-22 | train | 277 | 57 (21%) | 262 | 121 (46%) |
| 2021-22 | 2022-23 | train | 282 | 78 (28%) | 262 | 106 (40%) |
| 2022-23 | 2023-24 | validation | 273 | 80 (29%) | 253 | 81 (32%) |
| 2023-24 | 2024-25 | test (held out) | 277 | 89 (32%) | 262 | 39 (15%) |

Escalation has fewer usable rows than level because a relative change is
undefined where the current rate is zero.

Level prevalence drifts upward across the years rather than sitting at 20%.
That is the intended behaviour: the cutoff is a quantile of the training
years, not of the year being scored, so a genuinely worsening national
picture shows up as rising prevalence instead of being normalised away.

## What is not in the data

No personal or household-level records. These are published counts, so the
smallest unit anywhere in this project is a local authority in a year, or a
neighbourhood of roughly 1,500 people. Nothing here identifies a household,
and nothing here could.
